LAYER-BY-LAYER DETECTOR
How debris is physically caught after the IP collision
The Onion Model — Four Concentric Detector Layers
Every real collider detector (ATLAS, CMS, ALICE) wraps the IP in concentric cylindrical layers. Each layer is tuned to one job. A particle traverses all layers sequentially — its combined signature across layers gives its full identity.
Layer 1 — Silicon Tracker
Closest to IP. ~10 μm resolution silicon strip or pixel detectors. Charged particles ionise silicon atoms, leaving a hit. Chain of hits = track. Track curvature in the 4T magnetic field = momentum p = qBr. Charge sign from curve direction. Neutral particles: invisible here.
Layer 2 — ECAL (PbWO₄ crystals)
Electrons and photons initiate electromagnetic showers: e⁻ → e⁻γ → e⁻e⁺e⁻ → cascade. Total crystal light output = E_particle. Shower width ~1 Molière radius (~2 cm). Hadrons mostly pass through depositing only ~30% of energy.
Layer 3 — HCAL (Iron/Scintillator)
Hadrons (π±, p, K±, n) initiate hadronic showers: nuclear spallation cascade. Much wider and deeper than EM showers. Energy resolution ~50%/√E. Neutral hadrons (n, K⁰_L) first appear here — no tracker hit preceding the cluster.
Layer 4 — Muon Chambers
Only muons survive to here — they are 200× heavier than electrons so they don't shower. Drift tubes or resistive plate chambers give position measurements. A muon stub here + matching inner track = near-perfect muon ID with <0.1% fake rate.
Missing E_T: The vector sum of all measured transverse momenta. If non-zero, something invisible (ν, dark matter) escaped. Computed from calorimeter tower sums: MET = −Σ E_T(visible).

JET RECONSTRUCTION
Grouping quark/gluon sprays · Anti-kT algorithm · b-tagging · MET
Why Jets Exist
Quarks and gluons carry colour charge — they can never exist as free particles. The moment a quark flies away from the IP, the colour string stretching behind it carries enough energy to pop new quark-antiquark pairs from the vacuum. This hadronization cascade produces a collimated spray of hadrons called a jet — the observable proxy for the original parton.
Anti-kT: d_ij = min(k_Ti⁻², k_Tj⁻²) · ΔR²_ij/R²  |  ΔR=√(Δη²+Δφ²)  |  R≈0.4
Anti-kT Algorithm
Iteratively merges the hardest (highest pT) particles first, producing perfectly conical jets. Infrared and collinear safe — adding a soft particle or splitting one into two collinear particles doesn't change the jet boundary. The standard at LHC.
b-jet Tagging
B-mesons (b quark bound states) travel ~3mm before decaying. The silicon tracker resolves a secondary vertex displaced from the IP. Neural network b-taggers (DeepJet, ParticleNet) reach 70% efficiency with 1% fake rate using track impact parameters.
Tau Hadronic Jets
τ→hadrons + ν (~65% of decays). Narrow 1-prong or 3-prong jets. Isolated from QCD jets by: lower track multiplicity, smaller jet radius, isolation requirement (no nearby tracks outside core). Key for Higgs→ττ searches.
Missing E_T (MET)
Neutrinos and dark matter candidates leave no detector signal. Momentum conservation requires the transverse vector sum = 0. Anything missing = invisible particle. W→lν, supersymmetric neutralinos, and extra-dimensional gravitons all produce MET signatures.
INVARIANT MASS RECONSTRUCTION
Stacking collision events · Finding resonance peaks · Discovery
The Core Technique
If a particle X decays as X → A + B, then regardless of how fast X was moving when it decayed, the invariant mass M_X computed from daughters A and B is always the same — the rest mass of X. Plot M for thousands of collisions. Real particles produce a Gaussian peak above a smooth background continuum.
M²c⁴ = (E_A+E_B)² − |p⃗_A+p⃗_B|²c²  →  peak at M = m_X, width = Γ_X
Z Boson — 91.2 GeV
Reconstructed from e⁺e⁻ or μ⁺μ⁻ pairs. Γ=2.5 GeV. Used as detector energy-scale calibration. Appears every ~1000 collisions at LHC energies.
Higgs Boson — 125.1 GeV
H→γγ or H→ZZ→4ℓ. Γ=4 MeV (extremely narrow). Took ~10⁵ events and both ATLAS and CMS to reach 5σ. Announced July 4, 2012.
Top Quark — 173 GeV
t→Wb, reconstructed from W+b system. Only quark that decays before hadronizing (τ~10⁻²⁵s). Width Γ=1.4 GeV. Direct probe of Yukawa coupling.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS
Significance · p-values · Systematic errors · Discovery threshold
The 5σ Rule
Seeing a bump is not enough. The field demands that the probability of the signal being a random fluctuation of background is less than 1 in 3.5 million — a 5σ significance. This guards against the look-elsewhere effect: when you search many mass bins, some will fluctuate up by chance.
σ = N_signal / √N_background  |  5σ → p < 2.87×10⁻⁷  |  3σ → evidence (p < 1.35×10⁻³)
2σ — Fluctuation
1 in 22. Expected routinely by chance across many search bins. Publish nothing. Collect more data.
3σ — Evidence
1 in 740. Interesting. Report as "evidence for." Both experiments must confirm. Triggers targeted data-taking.
5σ — Discovery
1 in 3.5M. Gold standard. Requires independent confirmation. Higgs: ATLAS and CMS both at 5σ simultaneously.
Systematic Errors
Jet Energy Scale (JES)
Calorimeter response uncertainty ±1–5%. Propagates to all reconstructed masses. Calibrated using Z→jets pT balance and γ+jet events.
Pile-up
Multiple pp interactions per bunch crossing at high luminosity. Up to ~200 at HL-LHC. Pile-up jets subtracted using vertex association and jet area methods.
PDF Uncertainty
Parton distribution functions inside the proton have ~2–5% uncertainty. Limits precision of cross-section predictions. Constrained by HERA deep-inelastic scattering data.
GUF C_total
In the GUF model, C_total coherence fluctuations act as a systematic. A stable C_total → clean event selection. Spikes → increased background contamination in the mass plot.
